A Puzzleworld level

Pirate Seas

Seven lost pearls, an eel in an eyepatch, and a monster at the bottom of the sea who turns out to be afraid of a fish the size of your hand.

Designed by Benjamin — July 19, 2026

The setup

Pirate Seas runs backwards from every pirate story. Instead of taking treasure, Benjamin fills a chest with things he gathers for other people. A clam has lost seven pearls to other sea creatures, and getting them back is the spine of the level — which means finding out why each one was taken.

Pearls one, two, three

The shallows

The first two are easy: one sitting in an open shell, one being used as a ball by a little fish who'll hand it over if you play with her first. Then Benjamin spots a shark in the distance, chewing a pearl like a jawbreaker — and decides he isn't ready, and keeps swimming.

Pearl four

The eel

The fourth pearl belongs to an eel missing an eye, who's wearing it in the socket to hide being ugly. You can't just take it — he needs it. So you find him an eyepatch in a sunken ship, and he stops feeling ugly and starts feeling like a pirate, and hands the pearl over happily.

In his cave there's also a mirror. Benjamin borrows it.

Pearl five

The shark

Then he goes back. Remembering the fable of the fox and the cheese, he holds up the mirror and waits. The shark sees "another shark" with a pearl, decides he wants that one too, swims at it full speed, and knocks himself out cold. Benjamin grabs the pearl, sets the mirror on the shark's head to weigh him down, and speeds away.

Pearl six

The dolphin

The sixth is a breath: a dolphin who simply gives it back. She's not hiding anything or holding it hostage — she found it deep down and was already bringing it to him. On her way off she mentions, casually, that there's something crying down there.

Pearl seven

The bottom of the sea

There's been a storm on the horizon the whole level. It isn't weather. At the bottom of the trench is a baby kraken — bigger than the ship, completely alone, crying so hard it's been making the storm the entire time.

It's afraid of an anglerfish swallowing it. But down there it's pitch black, so all it's ever seen is the light on the lure — never the fish. The biggest creature in the game has spent its life hiding from something the size of your hand.

You can't outsmart it or hand it an object. You need light. So Benjamin swims back and retrieves the mirror off the shark's head, takes it down into the dark, and catches the anglerfish's own light and turns it around.

For one second everything is visible,
and it's a small fish with a silly light on a stick,
blinking in confusion.

The kraken stares for a long moment. Then it laughs, and the sound shakes the water. And that's when the storm stops — not when it gets brave, when it laughs. Miles above, the clouds break and the sea goes gold, and every creature Benjamin has met looks up at once.

The ending

The kraken mentions that someone used to keep it company down there — an old octopus in a cave, who stopped coming. Benjamin finds him: grumpy, yelling at everyone, his cave full of other people's lost things.

He wasn't stealing to be bad. Collecting was the only way he didn't feel alone. So instead of bringing him a thing, Benjamin brings him the creatures he took from — and they come and say hello. And that's when he hands over the last treasure.

What Benjamin worked out on his own

He chose to make the eyepatch easy to find, because:

"It would be too difficult for kids to have too many difficult puzzles and they'd get frustrated."

He set up the shark before the fight, so there'd be a worry humming under the eel puzzle. He built the eel's reward into the shark's solution. And he twice told me I was wrong about the structure — the octopus belongs at the end, not the beginning, and pearl six should be a dolphin — and he was right both times.
